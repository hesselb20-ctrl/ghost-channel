Data hidden in plain sight.

Ghost Channel hides a link, a message or a small file inside whatever a screen is showing. People see a normal picture. A phone camera sees the data. Both ends are ordinary web pages.

How it works

1

Each cell flickers, faintly and fast

The picture is divided into a grid. Every cell shifts slightly along the blue–yellow axis at 20 Hz, where eyes are least sensitive to change. Neighbouring quadrants move in opposite directions, so nothing pulses.

2

The frames add up to the original

On a 60 Hz screen the carrier is three frames long and those three shifts sum to zero in linear light. Averaged over time, which is what vision does at this speed, the picture is unchanged.

3

The camera turns it into a slow beat

A 30 fps camera cannot follow 20 Hz. It sees a 10 Hz beat instead, and the phase of that beat in each cell carries two bits. Reed–Solomon and a fountain code turn noisy cells into an exact file.

These rates come from the protocol itself. Decoding has been verified against a simulated rolling-shutter camera and in a browser loop-back, not yet on a physical phone and screen. The README lists every number with where it came from, and what still has to be measured by hand.

Built to be used in front of people

  • A visible mark by default. The sender shows a small “ghost” badge so an audience can know a screen is carrying data. A presenter can turn it off.
  • Nothing opens by itself. The receiver shows the full address of a link and waits for a tap. Text is shown as text. Files are only offered as downloads.
  • Flicker stays below what you can see. The debug mode that makes it visible carries a photosensitivity warning and an amplitude cap.

Standing on earlier work

Research groups showed that screens can carry hidden data, using native prototypes. Ghost Channel borrows their ideas and rebuilds them for what browsers can do.

  • HiLight (Dartmouth, MobiSys 2015): data in pixel translucency changes, on any content.
  • InFrame and InFrame++ (MobiSys 2015): complementary frame pairs inside full-frame video.
  • Uber-in-Light (Rutgers): modulation along a complementary colour axis.
  • Revelio (arXiv 2501.02349): shapes of pixel regions that survive rolling shutter and timing mismatch.
